# Mastercard Cybersecurity Virtual Experience Program

**Role simulated:** Security Analyst, Security Awareness team

Mastercard's simulation focuses on phishing — one of the most common threats organizations face — and demonstrates that effective security work doesn't always require a deep technical background, just an understanding of how attackers exploit human behavior.
 
### Task 1 — Design a Phishing Email Simulation
 
**Context:** Mastercard runs monthly phishing simulation campaigns to test staff and improve future training. A prior real phishing attempt against employees had failed because it was an obvious fake (mismatched sender domain, urgency-driven subject line, generic greeting).
 
**My task:** rebuild that "obvious fake" into a believable simulation email a real attacker might use — while keeping it ethical and internal, for staff training purposes only.
 
- Reviewed why the original email failed: a misspelled/non-Mastercard sender address, an unmasked raw link, generic phrasing, and urgency-based pressure tactics
- Rewrote the email applying the core principles of a convincing phishing attempt: added employee-relevant context, masked the malicious link within natural text, tightened spelling/grammar, and added markers of legitimacy (consistent branding cues, plausible IT department framing)
- Compared my draft against Mastercard's example "good" phishing email to identify further refinements

### Task 2 — Interpret Phishing Simulation Results
 
After the simulation ran, I was given campaign data broken down by team (email open rate, click-through rate, and phishing success rate — the percentage who clicked *and* submitted information).
 
**Key finding:** 
HR (75% phishing success rate) and Marketing (38% phishing success rate) were by far the most vulnerable teams 
well above the company-wide average of 18%, compared to teams like IT (0%) and Reception (0%) who showed strong resistance.
 
- Analyzed the dataset to rank departments by risk exposure, not just click-through rate alone
- Built a **3–5 slide security awareness presentation** targeted at HR and Marketing, explaining what phishing is, common attacker tactics, and how to spot red flags — designed to be visual and concise rather than a dense compliance lecture
- Benchmarked my presentation against an example built by a Mastercard Security Awareness professional, focused on contextual visual examples and clear action points
